# Google Open Source Community Guidelines

At Google, we recognize and celebrate the creativity and collaboration of open source contributors and the diversity of skills, experiences, cultures, and opinions they bring to the projects and environments they participate in.

Every one of Google's open source projects and spaces are inclusive environments, based on treating all individuals respectfully, regardless of gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, disabilities, neurodiversity, physical appearance, body size, ethnicity, nationality, race, age, religion, or similar personal characteristic.

We value diverse opinions, but we value respectful behavior more.

Respectful behavior includes:

* Being considerate, kind, constructive, and helpful.
* Not engaging in demeaning, discriminatory, harassing, hateful, sexualized, or physically threatening behavior, speech, and imagery.
* Not engaging in unwanted physical contact.

Some Google open source projects may adopt an explicit project governance policy, which may have additional detail as to specific roles in the governance of the particular project. Most Projects simply empower Project Maintainers, who are frequently Google employees, to serve as final decision-makers cleanly and fairly.

Finally, understand that rolling up your sleeves and participating in our Open Source projects doesn't make you an employee, spokesperson, or contractor of Google. So please remain honest about who you are, and don't represent yourself as speaking for Google (unless, of course, you actually are speaking for Google!).

## Resolve Peacefully

We know that sometimes people have differences of opinion and that sometimes people make mistakes. The key to resolving conflict is to assume good intentions, do your best to empathize with others, and always communicate kindly and constructively. Should you believe that someone is violating these guidelines, we ask that you bring it to the attention of the Project Maintainers or email opensource@google.com.

For the full guidelines, see <https://opensource.google/conduct/>.
